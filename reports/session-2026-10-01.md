## History-readout trial: result (`reports/memorise/real-history-readout/`)
A trial, never the locked check; the real network with the readout history on, everything else as the prereg-v5
real run (same starting weights, clips, schedule, scoring). **FAIL at step 3,000** (pass mark 0.9 on kick, snare and
closed hi-hat), but better than the locked readout on most drums. Final loss 0.3418 against 0.3497.

| Drum | History readout | prereg-v5 real (locked) | Control 1 (locked) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Kick** | **0.724** | 0.623 | 0.855 |
| Pedal hi-hat | 0.933 | 0.649 | 0.903 |
| **Snare** | **0.338** | 0.352 | 0.752 |
| **Closed hi-hat** | **0.601** | 0.520 | 0.880 |
| Open hi-hat | 0.708 | 0.621 | 0.830 |
| Toms | 0.541 | 0.588 | 0.774 |
| Crash | 0.391 | 0.500 | 0.667 |
| Ride | 0.507 | 0.520 | 0.556 |
| **All drums: found / false / missed, F1** | **481 / 518 / 193, 0.575** | 476 / 676 / 198, 0.521 | 606 / 213 / 68, 0.812 |

- **The gain is mostly fewer false hits** (518 against 676; real hits found about the same). Kick and closed hi-hat
  improve by about 0.1; precision rises (kick 0.50 to 0.61, closed hi-hat 0.39 to 0.49) with recall unchanged.
- **Snare doesn't improve** (0.338 against 0.352), nor do toms, crash and ride. That fits the trace: the wiring into
  the snare's motor neurons (wing power muscles) is narrow, and a readout can't recover information that isn't there.
- **It still trails control 1 by a wide margin** (all drums 0.575 against 0.812, under the locked readout for
  control 1). The history readout narrows the real network's gap on kick and closed hi-hat; it doesn't close it.
- **The learned profiles do use the past.** Each drum's hit profile (present; 5-50, 55-100, 105-150, 155-200 ms back):
  the present weight grew to 1.06-1.68, the nearer blocks are mostly positive, and the 105-150 ms block mostly
  negative (kick +1.26 +0.16 +0.11 -0.20 -0.47; closed hi-hat +1.12 +0.13 +0.28 +0.09 -0.40). A negative weight on
  the older past acts like "present minus recent past": it sharpens peaks, which fits the drop in false hits.
- **Readout inputs drifted again** (rms 6.1-22.0), so the prereg-v6 refit's trigger condition holds here too.
- Its progress, kick / snare / closed hi-hat at each check, against the locked run: level until step 1,000 (0.530 /
  0.238 / 0.457 against 0.500 / 0.226 / 0.420), then ahead on kick and closed hi-hat from step 1,250 to the end.

- **What it means:** the "before" window was the run at its hottest. The run started at 2.87 s a step while cool and
  slowed to about 3.10 s as the laptop heated up (the GPU dropping its clock at 86 °C). With the CPU capped, the GPU
  runs about 9 °C cooler, holds about 200 MHz more, and the steps are back to the cool-start speed. So the limits
  prevent the heat-up slowdown rather than adding new speed.
- **Side effect:** with the temperature limit below what the CPU reaches under load, the CPU sits at its 400 MHz
  minimum most of the time. Training doesn't care (the recorded step barely uses the CPU), but the F1 checks, the
  live chart's machine readings and the desktop are slower. Raise the limit for normal use.

### Power: more material per song or more controls (scripts/power_options.py)
The feasibility model's beat alignment on the 270 validation songs, hearing its own song against another band;
noise is one network's spread on the 151 test songs; "needs" is the true advantage for an 80% chance of winning,
as a share of the headroom, at control spreads of 0 / 0.115 / 0.23 of the headroom.

| Material per song | Controls | Headroom | Noise | False wins | Needs |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 clip | 5 | 5.8 | 1.12 | 5.8% | 63% / 73% / 98% |
| 2 clips | 5 | 6.1 | 1.20 | 5.8% | 64% / 74% / 98% |
| **4 clips (locked)** | **5** | **5.5** | **1.11** | **5.8%** | **66% / 76% / 99%** |
| Whole song | 5 | 6.4 | 1.11 | 5.6% | 57% / 68% / 94% |
| 4 clips | 10 | 5.5 | 1.11 | 2.0% | 72% / 83% / 109% |
| Whole song | 10 | 6.4 | 1.11 | 2.1% | 62% / 75% / 103% |

- **The noise doesn't shrink with more material** (about 1.1 points at every level), so it comes from differences
  between songs, which the fixed 151 test songs cap. Whole songs help a little (57% against 66% at no spread).
- **10 controls make false wins rarer (2%) but need a larger advantage**, because the best of 10 sits higher.
- **Neither is worth a change.** The limit stands: on beat alignment the main test can only reliably detect an
  advantage of most of what hearing the band is worth. Two reasons it is probably pessimistic: the feasibility
  model is weak and plays too few hits, and the noise came from two very different predictors (similar networks on
  the same songs should differ less).
- **For training:** the noise is fixed by the test songs; the signal is not. Networks that really learn from the
  band, and play about the right number of hits, open up more room. Worth watching hit density in training.
