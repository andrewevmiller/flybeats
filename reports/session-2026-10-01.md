### Power: more material per song or more controls (scripts/power_options.py)
The feasibility model's beat alignment on the 270 validation songs, hearing its own song against another band;
noise is one network's spread on the 151 test songs; "needs" is the true advantage for an 80% chance of winning,
as a share of the headroom, at control spreads of 0 / 0.115 / 0.23 of the headroom.

| Material per song | Controls | Headroom | Noise | False wins | Needs |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 clip | 5 | 5.8 | 1.12 | 5.8% | 63% / 73% / 98% |
| 2 clips | 5 | 6.1 | 1.20 | 5.8% | 64% / 74% / 98% |
| **4 clips (locked)** | **5** | **5.5** | **1.11** | **5.8%** | **66% / 76% / 99%** |
| Whole song | 5 | 6.4 | 1.11 | 5.6% | 57% / 68% / 94% |
| 4 clips | 10 | 5.5 | 1.11 | 2.0% | 72% / 83% / 109% |
| Whole song | 10 | 6.4 | 1.11 | 2.1% | 62% / 75% / 103% |

- **The noise doesn't shrink with more material** (about 1.1 points at every level), so it comes from differences
  between songs, which the fixed 151 test songs cap. Whole songs help a little (57% against 66% at no spread).
- **10 controls make false wins rarer (2%) but need a larger advantage**, because the best of 10 sits higher.
- **Neither is worth a change.** The limit stands: on beat alignment the main test can only reliably detect an
  advantage of most of what hearing the band is worth. Two reasons it is probably pessimistic: the feasibility
  model is weak and plays too few hits, and the noise came from two very different predictors (similar networks on
  the same songs should differ less).
- **For training:** the noise is fixed by the test songs; the signal is not. Networks that really learn from the
  band, and play about the right number of hits, open up more room. Worth watching hit density in training.
