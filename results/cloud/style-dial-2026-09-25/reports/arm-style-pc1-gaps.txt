ARM: style_pc1_gaps_cpu  (the pC1 style push, plus about half of every training clip silenced in 0.25-1 s gaps, with the drum targets kept)
Compared with the cloud baseline (velocity_probe_cpu), trained on the same machine, same data, seed and 12 epochs.

Does the style dial now change what it plays?
Somewhat, but less than the pC1 arm without gaps. Switching style changes 9% of the hits (baseline: 0%; pC1 alone: 20%). To a trained style, 11% change; to an unseen one, 7%. The largest signal shift from a style is 0.24 (baseline 0.002; pC1 alone 0.51). The gaps did not push the model to rely on style more. They roughly halved the effect.

Which drums play?
Snare and closed hat only, and the snare is 99% of the hits. That is close to the baseline's snare-only playing, and a step back from pC1 alone (five drums). Note that this model plays at a threshold of 0.4, not 0.5, because training picked that as its best.

Timing score (best val onset F): 0.2765 (baseline 0.2834, so 0.007 lower; pC1 alone 0.2804).

Groove and busyness: groove match to the human is 0.30 (baseline 0.29). It plays 9.8 hits a second, the human 8.6 (baseline 10.1). Bar-to-bar variety is back near the baseline: two random bars differ in 18% of their hits (baseline 17%, human 32%).

Sliders: drive now changes only 4% of hits (pC1 alone: 10-11%). Tightness 1.5 silences it again: 98% of hits change and it keeps 1% of them, as in the baseline. Pocket changes 11-12%.

Other kits (kit check): holds on the other E-GMD kits (0.284 vs 0.285 on its own kit). On held-out kits it drops 0.011 (0.274), a little less than the baseline's 0.015 drop.
