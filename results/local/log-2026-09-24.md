## Why the velocity head ignores the motor signal: it does not converge

Plain-language version: `runs\queue\reports\why-the-loudness-guess-ignores-the-signal.txt`.
Scripts: `results\local\diag-2026-09-24\grad_share.py` (instruments real
training steps) and `head_refit.py` (closed-form head refits). Both are
single-threaded (`torch.set_num_threads(1)`, run with `OMP_NUM_THREADS=1`), so
they ran beside the seed-2 training. `head_refit.py` takes about 4 min.

1. **The heads sit at init.** Cosine similarity between arms' masked
   `vel_readout.weight` rows is ≥ 0.987 (A′3 vs baseline: 0.999–1.0), and the
   weight RMS is 0.10–0.16 against an init std of 0.123. The sigmoid heads'
   biases stay at 0.0 ± 0.1 and the linear head's at 0.5 ± 0.03, while the
   target mean at hits is 0.59. The onset readout barely moves either (bias
   -2.0 ± 0.1). A′3 ≈ baseline because they share the seed-0 init.
2. **The gradient is not starved.** `grad_share.py`, 6 real steps from a fresh
   init: the global norm is 0.47–0.74, so `grad_clip` 1.0 never binds. The
   vel_head grad norm is 0.12–0.31, the largest of any group per parameter.
   AdamW moves it about 1e-3 per step, but the direction is not consistent.
3. **Same function class, fit in closed form on train peaks, scored on val
   peaks** (`ls_mask`: masked, raw rates, linear, λ = 1e-3):

```
class      hits  head   ls_mask [clip CI]      ls_supp  ridge_all  mean²/var  top-sv
kick        266  -0.08  +0.11 [-0.04,+0.26]    +0.08    +0.11        64      0.74
snare       615  +0.08  +0.39 [+0.27,+0.49]    +0.27    +0.37        66      0.59
hat_closed  309  +0.07  -0.06 [-0.17,+0.04]    -0.06    -0.06        49      0.61
hat_open     55  +0.16  +0.06 [-0.19,+0.33]    +0.11    +0.22        79      0.63
tom_low     194  -0.04  +0.30 [+0.14,+0.46]    +0.18    +0.37        65      0.56
tom_mid     168  +0.12  +0.34 [+0.16,+0.49]    +0.47    +0.41        56      0.66
crash       123  +0.33  +0.12 [-0.10,+0.32]    +0.06    +0.10        71      0.58
```
   (baseline checkpoint. `ls_supp` = fit on support steps weighted by y, i.e.
   train.py's own objective. `ridge_all` = standardised ridge on all 66 units,
   train-fit.) The hemisphere mask costs little (`ls_mask` ≈ `ridge_all`), and
   the support-weighted objective is not the problem. **This is an
   optimisation failure.**
4. **The likely cause is conditioning.** The motor rates at hits have
   Σmean² / Σvar ≈ 50–80, and one centred direction holds 56–74% of the
   variance. Adam fits the common mode and crawls along the informative
   low-variance directions while the RNN moves the features underneath it.
   Corollary: **`velocity_weight` is nearly a no-op for the head's own
   parameters under Adam** (it is scale-invariant apart from eps). It only
   changes the pull on shared RNN params, which is why A′1 could not fix the head.
5. **Prediction for seed 2:** the per-class head verdicts will move with the
   init. crash "right" everywhere may be partly an init artefact
   (head +0.33 > `ls_mask` +0.12).

Options, not done:
(a) post-hoc closed-form refit of `vel_readout` on train peaks for each saved
    checkpoint, then rescore (minutes, CPU);
(b) a fixed affine standardisation of the motor rates before both readouts,
    calibrated once pre-training like `calibrate_encoder`; still one linear
    layer, then retrain;
(c) a separate, higher lr param group for the decoder.
No code edited for this.

### (a) `scripts/refit_velocity_head.py`: new, done for all four arms

Loads a checkpoint and runs the frozen network over the train split. It
takes one row per hit (`hit_peaks`, y > 0.95) and solves `vel_readout` per
class through its hemisphere mask. The solve is ridge (λ = 1) in
standardised coordinates, folded back into raw-rate weights, so it drops into
an unmodified `nn.Linear`. A sigmoid head is fit on logit targets and then
refined by damped Gauss-Newton on MSE in 0..1. Classes with < 30 train hits
keep the trained weights (tom_high: 3 hits). Only the two `vel_readout`
tensors change, and the checkpoint gains a `refit` dict. `--out` must differ
from `--checkpoint`. Validation is never touched.

Outputs: `runs/refit/<arm>/best.pt`, `refit_<arm>.txt` (per-class train r),
`probe_<arm>.txt` (fixed probe) and `SUMMARY.txt`. Report:
`runs/queue/reports/refit-loudness-readouts.txt`. Everything ran with
OMP_NUM_THREADS=1, about 4.5 min per arm, beside the seed-2 training.

### (b) `kit.standardize_motor`: code in, experiment queued

- `src/decoder.py`: `MotorToDrums(standardize_motor=False)`. When True it
  registers buffers `motor_mean`, `motor_scale` and `motor_calibrated`
  (identity until calibrated), and `_inputs()` applies `(r - mean) / scale`
  before **both** `forward` and `velocity`. `calibrate(rates)` uses per-unit
  mean/std over all steps; a unit with std < 1e-6 keeps scale 1. It returns
  stats including `level_to_sd` (Σmean²/Σvar).
- `src/train.py`: `calibrate_decoder(model, dataset, cfg, device)` runs the
  same 16 seeded clips as `calibrate_encoder` through the calibrated encoder
  and the untrained core, with genre tonic, in eval mode, and restores the
  RNG. It is called in `main` right after `calibrate_encoder`. It is a
  **no-op and consumes no RNG when the flag is off**, so flag-off runs are
  unchanged.
- `src/ablations.py`: calls `calibrate_decoder` per arm after
  `calibrate_encoder` (each arm calibrates on its own core, like the rate
  ceiling).
- `src/build.py` passes `kit.standardize_motor`. `src/bundle.py` enables it
  when `decoder.motor_mean` is in the bundle state, so old bundles load
  unchanged. realtime/probe/diagnose all go through `decoder()` and

## Kit check: does timing survive other drum kits? (~14:10–14:25)

**Data.** The E-GMD 4-kit subset is on disk at `data/egmd/e-gmd-subset/`
(`scripts/fetch_egmd_subset.py`). It holds 3,756 recordings, 7.9 GB downloaded
and 2.6 GB stored as 22.05 kHz FLAC, with 0 failures. Each train performance
is on 4 seeded kits. Each validation and test performance is on 1 kit train can
use (`<split>`) and 1 held-out kit (`<split>_unseen_kit`). The held-out kits are
808 Simple, Bigga Bop (Jazz), Speed Metal, Cassette (Lo-Fi Compress) and
Unplugged. Nothing has trained on it.

**Method.** `scripts/kit_check.py` (tests: `tests/test_kit_check.py`). E-GMD's
MIDI is identical to GMD's for the same performance: same notes, same times,
zero offset, same audio length. So the check scores each checkpoint on the
**same 2 s passages** (up to 8 per performance) through three kit sounds, and
the kit sound is the only thing that changes. The score is per-window
`metrics.onset_f_sweep`, averaged within each performance, then across
performances. Differences are paired per performance, with 95% ranges from
resampling performances. Reports: `runs/kit_check/<run>-<split>/report.txt`,
about 4 min each at 4 threads.

```
timing score at the checkpoint's threshold    GMD kit   other kits (paired diff)          held-out kits (paired diff)
baseline s0, validation (thr 0.5)             0.288     0.286 (-0.003 [-.015,+.008]) ?    0.274 (-0.014 [-.024,-.005]) WORSE
baseline s0, test       (thr 0.5)             0.266     0.256 (-0.010 [-.023,+.001]) ?    0.259 (-0.006 [-.019,+.005]) ?
std s0,      validation (thr 0.6)             0.328     0.278 (-0.050 [-.067,-.033]) WORSE 0.272 (-0.057 [-.076,-.037]) WORSE
```
Held-out kits one by one, std s0: 808 −0.079, jazz −0.092, lo-fi −0.012, speed
metal −0.037, unplugged −0.074 (19–28 performances each).

What it says:
- **The baseline barely notices other kits.** It loses −0.003 to −0.014, and
  only the validation held-out loss is confident. It plays snare-only
  (`runs/variety/velocity_probe_cpu/report.txt`), so it has little
  kit-specific skill to lose.
- **std's timing gain is kit-specific.** On GMD's kit it beats the baseline by
  +0.040 (0.328 vs 0.288 under this protocol). On other kits it drops back to
  the baseline's level (0.278 vs 0.286, and 0.272 vs 0.274). The paired losses,
  −0.050 and −0.057, are confident.
- **It is not a volume shift.** With each kit allowed its own best threshold,
  E-GMD recovers only 0.001 (0.279 at 0.5, against std's own 0.6).
- So "`standardize_motor` is the result of the day" still holds for loudness,
  and for timing **on the TD-11 kit it trained on**. The timing gain does not
  transfer to other kits. One seed, one checkpoint.
- Implication: kit sensitivity grows as the model gets better at GMD. That
  raises E-GMD training's priority once the loudness fix and Phase B′ settle.
  E-GMD training still needs the one-kit-per-performance sampler, which is not
  built.

Suggestion: run `kit_check.py` on every new checkpoint beside the probe. It is
cheap, and it would have caught this at once.

Protocol note: these scores average several windows per performance, so they
sit a little above `train.py`'s one-window-per-clip numbers (0.2834 → 0.288,
0.3207 → 0.328). Compare within this table, not across to the training logs.
