WHY THE MODEL'S LOUDNESS GUESS IGNORES INFORMATION IT HAS
Written 24 September 2026
==========================================================================

SHORT ANSWER
The part of the model that makes the loudness guess never finishes learning.
The information it needs is there, and a guess of exactly the same simple
kind can use it. Training just doesn't get it there in 12 rounds. This is a
training problem, not a hearing problem, and it is probably cheap to fix.

WHAT I CHECKED
1. Is the loudness part being trained at all?  Yes, but it hardly moves.
   After training, its settings are still almost exactly where they started
   at random. In all four arms they match each other almost perfectly (99%
   and up), and they are the same size as at the start. That is also why A3
   came out nearly identical to the baseline: both started from the same
   random settings and neither moved far from them.
2. Is some safety limit starving it of updates?  No. Training caps how big
   each update can be, but the cap never kicked in, and the loudness part
   gets some of the largest updates in the whole model. They just point in
   different directions from one step to the next and mostly cancel out.
3. Could the same kind of guess do better?  Yes, a lot better. I fitted
   the same simple kind of guess (same inputs, same one-sided wiring) in one
   exact calculation on the training songs, then scored it on the test songs:

     drum       trained guess   same kind, fitted exactly
     snare          +0.08        +0.39   RIGHT (confident) [+0.27 to +0.49]
     low tom        -0.04        +0.30   RIGHT (confident) [+0.14 to +0.46]
     mid tom        +0.12        +0.34   RIGHT (confident) [+0.16 to +0.49]
     kick           -0.08        +0.11   can't tell        [-0.04 to +0.26]
     closed hat     +0.07        -0.06   can't tell
     crash          +0.33        +0.12   can't tell

   Fitted the way training actually grades it (across the whole hit, not
   only its peak), it still reaches +0.27, +0.18 and +0.47 on those three
   drums. So what training aims for is fine. The problem is getting there.

WHY TRAINING STALLS
The signals the loudness part reads are nearly the same size all the time,
with only tiny wobbles on top. The steady part is about 50 to 80 times
bigger than the wobbles, and the loudness information is in the wobbles.
A learner taking small steps spends its effort matching the steady part and
barely inches along the wobbles. The same thing is happening to the timing
guess, which also barely left its starting point.

This also explains why A1 (loudness counts 5x) could not help. The learning
method used here adjusts its step sizes automatically, so making the
loudness grade count five times as much barely changes how the loudness part
itself learns. It mostly just pulls harder on the rest of the model.

A PREDICTION THE SEED-2 RUNS CAN TEST
If the loudness part mostly stays where it started, the second-seed runs,
which start from different random settings, should give different
drum-by-drum verdicts, and those verdicts should be about as much a matter of
luck as of the change being tested. Crash being "right" in every arm may be
partly luck of this kind too: the exact fit above does worse on crash than
the trained guess does.

WHAT WOULD FIX IT (Andrew's call; nothing has been changed)
  a. Quickest, no retraining: refit just the loudness part of each saved
     model with the exact calculation above, and rescore. Minutes on CPU.
  b. Proper fix: even out the signals before the loudness part reads them
     (subtract their usual level and scale their wobbles up). It is still
     one simple straight-line readout, measured once before training starts,
     the same way the input side is already calibrated. Then retrain.
  c. Also possible: give the two final readouts their own, larger step size.
