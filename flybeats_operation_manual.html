flybeats Training

The operation manual — local compute, phase by phase

There's no drum sound and no fly on screen while this runs. Just a step counter, a loss number, and a few files on disk growing on a schedule. Here's what those are supposed to do, and what it looks like when they don't.

What "training" physically is

Forget neurons and synapses for a second. On your machine, training is one loop, repeated thousands of times.

  1. Grab a small chunk of audio and its matching drum MIDI.
  2. Run it forward through the network — get a guess.
  3. Compare the guess to the real MIDI to get a single number, the loss. Lower is better.
  4. Work out which direction to nudge every trainable number (edge gains, time constants, thresholds) to make that loss slightly smaller.
  5. Nudge them a tiny bit. Go back to step one.

That's it. There is no drum sound, no picture, no fly avatar during training — that's what realtime.py is for later. "Watching training" means watching numbers scroll in a terminal, plus one or two files on disk growing on a schedule. If nothing is printing and nothing is growing, something is wrong even without an error message.

loss

Is the model getting better at the task?

still running

Is the process alive, or did it silently die or hang?

Where things break, in order

Problems surface in the order the pipeline runs. You won't hit a Phase 3 problem until Phases 0 and 1 are clean — use that to narrow down what broke.

gate

Phase 1
subgraph

Subgraph extraction

A reported node count in the 10k–30k target — and every motor neuron type you care about is still reachable from JO afferents inside that subgraph.
SymptomLikely causeWhat to do
Subgraph is way over 30kk-hop radius set too largeReduce hop count before touching anything downstream

Phase 2
enc / dec

Encoder and decoder

Low risk by design — both are thin linear layers on top of fixed DSP. The only fail state here is a shape mismatch: a dimension-mismatch error at the very first forward pass, immediately, loudly, at step 0. Get past step 0 and this phase is fine.

Phase 3
training

The training loop

Where most of your attention goes. A healthy run: a step counter incrementing at a roughly steady speed, loss trending down over the first few hundred–thousand steps (noisy, not flat, not a wall of nan), mean firing rate sitting in a healthy middle range, and a checkpoint file that actually updates on schedule.

SymptomLikely causeWhat to check
Crash within the first few steps, dimension/device errorShape mismatch, or a tensor left on CPU while the model is on GPURead the traceback — it names the exact tensor and shape; fix and rerun
CUDA out of memory, usually earlyBatch size × sequence length too big for VRAM, even with checkpointing/bf16 onLower batch size first, then the truncated-BPTT window length, before assuming the subgraph is too big
Loss becomes nan or inf after some stepsLearning rate too high, or per-edge log-gain initialization blew upAdd/check gradient clipping; the step right before the first nan is the one to inspect
Loss decreasing, but firing-rate stats pin at 0 or ceilingThe firing-rate regularizer isn't strong enough — the network is "solving" the loss by going silent or saturatingWatch mean firing rate as its own metric, not just loss. If it's pinned, increase the regularizer weight
Loss flatlines immediately, no crashGradients aren't flowing — usually edge magnitudes got frozen along with the signsConfirm which tensors have requires_grad = True; log the gradient norm — zero means nothing is updating
Loss looks great, output sounds like near-silence or noiseClass imbalance — most timesteps have no drum hit, so "always predict nothing" gets a deceptively low lossLook at per-class precision/recall, not just aggregate loss — a known pattern in sparse-onset tasks, not a bug
Steps/sec slowly worsens, no crash yetMemory/graph leak — usually a recurrent hidden state that never got detached between truncated-BPTT windowsIf steps/sec is dropping and memory use is climbing, this is almost always the cause
Process stops updating, no crash, no errorHung on a blocking I/O call, or genuinely deadlockedCheck whether it's still burning CPU/GPU before deciding it's dead
checkpointing, specifically "Training is running" and "checkpoints are being written" are two different facts — verify both. A run that trains fine but never successfully writes a checkpoint (permissions, disk full, wrong path) wastes all its progress the moment it stops.
Phase 4
ablations

Ablation controls

Same training loop, run four times with the topology or signs changed. The fail state here is subtler than a crash.

topology isn't actually constraining anything The random-rewire ablation scores almost identically to the real connectome. Suspicious — suggests a dense fallback path got used by accident somewhere in the code, since the whole point of this experiment is that topology should matter.
a fake result, self-inflicted One ablation "fails to converge" while the others train fine. Before concluding anything about the connectome, check that all four runs used identical hyperparameters — a silently different learning rate or batch size is the most common way to manufacture this.
Phase 5
realtime

Realtime playback

A different failure family entirely — audio and timing issues, not training issues.

  • Buffer underrun/overrun → audible glitches, a dropped-frame counter climbing
  • Inference taking longer than the latency budget → drift between audio and drum output that worsens over time, not a one-off delay
  • MIDI never fires → check the gate threshold logic; the master rhythm gate is near-binary, so a small threshold or lesion bug can silently zero it out

How to actually launch a run

The full sequence, in the order it needs to happen — plus the two habits that make an hours-long run survivable.

3a. One-time environment setup

What a virtual environment is, and why it matters here: it's an isolated folder holding its own private copy of Python plus whatever packages you install into it. Without one, pip install writes into your system-wide Python, where it can silently collide with unrelated projects or conflict with versions something else on your machine needs. flybeats's dependencies should live in their own bubble, separate from everything else.

Creating one (run once, from the flybeats/ project folder):

python3 -m venv .venv

This creates a .venv folder next to your code. Using conda instead: conda create -n flybeats python=3.11.

Activating it — this is the step that actually "turns it on" for your current terminal session, and it's platform-specific:

macOS / Linux: source .venv/bin/activate Windows PowerShell: .venv\Scripts\Activate.ps1 Windows Command Prompt: .venv\Scripts\activate.bat conda instead of venv: conda activate flybeats

How to confirm it worked: your prompt should grow a (.venv) prefix. As a second check, which python (macOS/Linux) or where python (Windows) should point inside the .venv folder — not a system Python path. If it points anywhere else, activation didn't take, and every install you run next goes to the wrong place.

Install into the activated environment:

pip install neuprint-python connectorch pip install sounddevice mido python-rtmidi # only needed once you reach Phase 5
two traps that look like unrelated bugs later Every new terminal window or tab starts deactivated — a sudden ModuleNotFoundError tomorrow is almost always a forgotten activation, not a broken install. A new tmux session doesn't inherit activation either; activate again inside it before launching anything.

To leave the environment when you're done: deactivate (or conda deactivate).

Check what hardware you actually have before launching anything: nvidia-smi. No GPU, or the command errors out? You're on CPU — much slower, not broken. Then set your token, in the same terminal session that will run the scripts: export NEUPRINT_TOKEN=...

3b. Launch order — don't skip ahead

  1. Gate: python scripts/verify_types.py → confirm data/verified_types.json is populated.
  2. Subgraph: python src/subgraph.py --config configs/v1_8piece.yaml → check the reported node count and reachability.
  3. Smoke test — do this before any real run, on the smallest config:
    python src/train.py --config configs/v1_3piece_sanity.yaml --max-steps 200
    The goal isn't good results — it's confirming the loop runs start to finish in minutes, not hours.
  4. Real launch, once the smoke test passes clean:
    python src/train.py --config configs/v1_8piece.yaml
a flag not to reach for by accident --full-graph is the offline, single-batch render mode on the complete 164k-node graph — comparison/demo only. Regular training always runs on the extracted 10k–30k subgraph.

3c. What happens in the seconds after you hit enter

In order: config loads → subgraph loads into memory → model builds (edge signs freeze, gains initialize to log(synapse_count)) → first batch is pulled → forward pass → backward pass → the first loss number prints. Anything that crashes before that first loss number is an environment, shape, or device problem — not a training-dynamics problem.

3d. Running it somewhere that survives you closing the laptop

Real runs take hours to days. A plain terminal command dies the moment you close that terminal. Two easy ways around it:

nohup python src/train.py --config configs/v1_8piece.yaml > run.log 2>&1 & disown
tmux new -s flybeats # run the command inside, then detach: Ctrl-b d # reattach any time later: tmux attach -t flybeats

Either way, don't just trust that it's alive — ps aux | grep train.py, or a glance at nvidia-smi for GPU utilization, confirms it's actually working and not a zombie process.

3e. Resuming vs. starting fresh

Fresh start: no resume flag at all. Resume:

python src/train.py --config configs/v1_8piece.yaml --resume checkpoints/latest.pt

This restores weights, optimizer state, and step count. A small loss bump right after resuming is normal — only worry if it looks like the NaN or plateau patterns above.

3f. Launching the four Phase 4 ablation runs

  • Same entrypoint, one config/flag difference per run: python src/ablations.py --mode random_rewire --config configs/ablation_random_rewire.yaml
  • One GPU: run the four sequentially — four training loops at once gets you an OOM, or all four crawling.
  • Multiple GPUs: pin each explicitly — CUDA_VISIBLE_DEVICES=0 python ..., CUDA_VISIBLE_DEVICES=1 python ... — so they don't contend for the same device.
  • Keep the four launches identical except the one ablation flag. A single shared base config with only that flag overridden is the easiest way to avoid drifting a hyperparameter between runs.

The two decision points — types confirmed, subgraph reachable — are the gates from Phases 0 and 1. Everything past them is the training loop and its monitor/kill/resume cycle.

How to actually see training progress

Worth saying up front: the point-cloud and fly-avatar visualization in flybeats_demo.html is not a training monitor — it's mocked-up UI for a trained model performing in realtime. During Phase 3 you'll see numbers in a terminal or a browser dashboard, fed by an entirely separate logging mechanism.

Tier 0 — print statements

Zero setup. The script prints step, loss, maybe grad norm and firing-rate mean, every N steps. Foreground: watch it scroll. Background:

python train.py > run.log 2>&1 & tail -f run.log

Fine for a quick sanity check; bad past a few minutes — no curve, no side-by-side ablation comparison.

Tier 1 — TensorBoard

Recommended default. Fully local, no account. Your script writes a few numbers per log interval to a runs/ folder; view with tensorboard --logdir runs/ at http://localhost:6006.

Put each ablation run in its own subfolder and TensorBoard overlays all four on one chart automatically — exactly the Phase 4 comparison you need. Track: loss, firing-rate mean/min/max, gradient norm, steps/sec, and per-class onset F-measure once you have an eval pass.

Tier 2 — Weights & Biases

Same idea, hosted, one extra API-key step. Its edge here: it builds the side-by-side run-comparison table for you, mapping directly onto the Phase 4 ablation table without assembling it by hand. Trade-off: your metrics leave your machine.

Reading the curves

Mapping shapes back to the fail states from the phase map above.

What the chart showsWhat it means
Loss line noisy but sloping down, no vertical jumpsHealthy
Loss curve breaks off, or a literal nan point appearsThe NaN/Inf failure — go to the step right before the gap
Firing-rate line goes flat, pinned top or bottom, while loss still looks fineSaturation failure — this is why firing rate needs its own chart, not something inferred from loss alone
Loss line is perfectly flat from step 1, never movesFrozen-gradient failure — cross-check a gradient-norm chart sitting at 0
Steps/sec trending downward over hours while loss looks otherwise normalLeak / undetached-hidden-state failure
logging cadence vs. checkpoint cadence Two different schedules, easy to conflate. Log interval (every 10–50 steps): cheap scalar writes, fine to do often. Checkpoint interval (every N minutes or every epoch): the actual saved snapshot, bigger, meant to be less frequent. Logging often and checkpointing rarely is normal and correct — too tight a checkpoint interval is a common, self-inflicted way to slow down steps/sec via disk I/O.

Quick reference: what to watch, when

Time since startingWhat you should seeIf you don't, suspect
First 30 secondsProcess starts, first step completes, no immediate crashShape/device mismatch at the Phase 2/3 boundary
First few minutesLoss printing every step, steps/sec roughly steady, no OOMBatch size / sequence length too large for VRAM
First hourLoss trending down with noise; firing-rate stats not pinned at extremes; at least one checkpoint writtenRegularizer weight, frozen-gradient bug, or a dead subgraph from Phase 1
Several hoursSteps/sec still steady; loss still improving or sensibly plateauedMemory leak (undetached hidden state) if steps/sec is falling
End of an ablation suiteAll four runs used identical hyperparameters; results differ in a coherent storySilently mismatched configs across ablation runs

When to kill a run vs. let it keep going

kill immediately

Any crash, nan/inf in the loss, OOM.

let it run, watch closely

Loss flat for a while early on (rate-based LIF models can have a slow start); firing rates drifting toward an extreme but not pinned yet.

kill and investigate before restarting

Firing rates fully pinned for many consecutive checkpoints, steps/sec steadily degrading, or a checkpoint file that hasn't updated in far longer than its save interval.

Don't trust "it's still going" alone — a hung process and a training process both look like nothing new happened. Confirm actual GPU/CPU activity, not just log silence, before deciding which one you have.
